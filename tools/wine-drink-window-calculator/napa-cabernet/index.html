The window has just opened — drinking well now, and should improve further.

Windows are broad guides for well-made, well-stored examples of each style — producer, vintage quality and storage all shift them.

What sets the window

Napa’s warm, dry growing season brings Cabernet Sauvignon to full ripeness almost every year, so the wines are generous, often above fourteen and a half per cent alcohol, with plenty of tannin and a good deal of new French oak. Ripe fruit and alcohol make them approachable earlier than Bordeaux, which is why the window here opens at five years after the vintage and runs to twenty. Mountain sites such as Howell Mountain, Spring Mountain and Mount Veeder give firmer, longer-lived wines than the valley floor, and producers who pick earlier for freshness tend to make bottles that age more gracefully than the ripest styles.

Opened too early

In its first few years, Napa Cabernet tastes of blackberry jam, cassis, mocha and vanilla, with tannin that is ripe but thick and a warmth of alcohol on the finish. It is pleasurable but one-dimensional, and the oak can read as sweetness.

In the window

Time trims the sweetness of the fruit and folds the oak in. Mature examples show dark fruit alongside cedar, dried herbs, tobacco, graphite and earth, with tannin that has gone from chewy to supple. The best keep a freshness that surprises people who know the wine only young, and the mountain wines in particular take on a savoury, almost Bordeaux-like profile.

Frequently asked questions

Can you age Napa Cabernet?

Yes. Although it is often drunk young, a well-made Napa Cabernet from a good producer has the tannin and concentration to improve for a decade or more, and mountain-grown wines and the more restrained styles can go further. The riper, lower-acid bottlings tend to plateau earlier and lose freshness first, so it is worth tasting one before committing a case to long cellaring.

Should I decant Napa Cabernet?

A young one, yes: an hour or two in a decanter softens the tannin and lets the oak and fruit settle. A mature bottle needs less air and more care, since it may have thrown sediment, so stand it upright beforehand and decant just before serving. Serving slightly below room temperature, around 16–18 °C, also keeps the alcohol in check.
