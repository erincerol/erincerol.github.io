When should I drink Rioja Gran Reserva?

Rioja Gran Reserva spends years in barrel and bottle before it is sold, so it reaches you partly aged, and good examples still have decades ahead.

Windows are broad guides for well-made, well-stored examples of each style — producer, vintage quality and storage all shift them.

Aged before it leaves the bodega

Gran Reserva must age for at least five years before release, with at least two of those in oak barrels and the rest in bottle, and bodegas usually make it only in vintages they judge good enough. Because the window is counted from the vintage, the opening figure of eight years is often just two or three years after the wine reaches the shelf, and the window runs to twenty-five. Tempranillo brings moderate tannin and good acidity; Graciano and Mazuelo, where used, add colour, acid and grip. Traditional houses ageing in old American oak make wines that already show secondary character at release, while modern producers using French oak and riper fruit make denser wines that need longer.

Opened too early

Even after five years of ageing, a Gran Reserva opened on release can taste of the barrel first: coconut, dill and vanilla from American oak, or toast and clove from French, with the fruit tucked behind and the tannin still a little dusty.

In the window

Mature Gran Reserva is translucent and brick-tinged, with dried cherry and strawberry, leather, tobacco, cinnamon and a savoury, almost meaty edge. The oak becomes a background of sweet spice rather than a flavour of its own. Good examples from traditional houses keep that delicacy for a very long time.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Rioja Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva?

The terms describe minimum ageing before release. Crianza must age for two years, at least one in barrel; Reserva for three years, at least one in barrel; Gran Reserva for five years, at least two in barrel. Many producers go well beyond those minimums. Longer ageing does not automatically mean a better wine, but Gran Reserva is usually made from the bodega’s best fruit in its better vintages.

Should I decant old Rioja?

Mature Gran Reserva often throws a light sediment, so decanting it gently just before serving is sensible. Traditional styles, already well aged by the bodega, need little air and can lose their delicate aromas if left too long; a quick decant and a large glass are usually enough. Modern, more extracted Gran Reservas benefit from an hour or two to soften.
