Why these wines work with fish

Fish is delicate and often lean, and the old rule of white wine with fish holds for good reasons. Tannin in red wine can react with fish oils and leave a metallic taste, while acidity does what a squeeze of lemon does: it lifts the flavour and freshens the palate. So the default is a dry white with freshness, and the list here is mostly that: Sauvignon Blanc from Sancerre or Marlborough, Albarino from Rías Baixas, Chablis, Assyrtiko, Vermentino, Gruner Veltliner. Match the weight of the wine to the fish and its sauce. Sole, plaice and other white fish simply cooked want something light and crisp; a meaty fillet of turbot, hake or cod in a butter sauce can take a rounder white Burgundy or a Chenin Blanc from Vouvray. Oily fish such as mackerel, sardines and salmon carry more flavour and fat, and they are where light, low-tannin reds become possible: Pinot Noir, a pale Etna red from Sicily, Mencia, Cinsault or a lightly chilled Chinon. Smoked fish suits an aromatic or off-dry white, Riesling being the obvious choice, and a cold, dry sherry is an underrated partner for anything fried. Champagne and other sparkling wines cut through batter and fat with ease, and a cold, pale rosé from Provence works wherever you would otherwise reach for a crisp white.
