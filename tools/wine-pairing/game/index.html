Why these wines work with game

Game tastes of where it lived. Venison, pheasant, grouse, pigeon and wild duck carry earthy, iron-rich and sometimes slightly high flavours that make bright, fruit-forward wine taste simple and sweet beside them. What works is something savoury with a little age: a wine that has moved past primary fruit into forest floor, mushroom, leather and dried cherry, so that it echoes the meat rather than shouting over it. Pinot Noir is the natural partner for feathered game, and a red Burgundy or a Côte de Nuits with some depth is about as good as it gets with roast partridge or duck. Nebbiolo does the same job with more structure; a Barolo or Barbaresco given air before dinner has the tannin for venison and the tar-and-roses perfume for a rich game pie. Most game is lean, so tannin has little fat to soften it, which is why age matters: mellowed tannin will not dry the meat out. Furred game and slow braises can take darker wines such as Syrah from Côte-Rôtie, Mourvedre or a Priorat, especially when juniper, red wine or berries are in the sauce. Sweet accompaniments such as redcurrant jelly nudge you towards riper fruit, perhaps a Sonoma Pinot. Whatever you choose, keep the oak quiet; heavy new oak masks exactly the savoury notes that make the pairing work.
