Why these wines work with pasta and rice

Pasta and rice dishes are mostly about the sauce, and the wines that suit them best are something Italian and easy-going. Italian reds grew up alongside tomato, olive oil and hard cheese, so they tend to have high acidity and moderate tannin, which is exactly what a tomato sauce needs. Tomato is sharp; a wine with less acidity than the sauce will taste flat and flabby beside it. Sangiovese, whether a Chianti or something simpler, is the default for a ragù or a pizza, Barbera does the same with softer tannin, and Dolcetto and Montepulciano are relaxed, everyday reds that never get in the way. Richer dishes such as lasagne, a slow beef ragù or a mushroom risotto can take more structure: Nebbiolo from Barolo or Barbaresco, or a Valpolicella. Cream, butter and seafood change the colour of the glass. Carbonara, spaghetti with clams, seafood risotto and pasta with pesto all suit a crisp Italian white, Pinot Grigio, Vermentino, Trebbiano or Garganega, the grape behind Soave. Rice dishes from around the Mediterranean follow the same logic, with Bobal, Xinomavro, Agiorgitiko and Okuzgozu playing the part the Italian reds play at home. Keep it simple; this is a family that rewards a wine you can drink by the glass rather than one you have to think about.
