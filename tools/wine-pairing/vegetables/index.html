Why these wines work with vegetables

Vegetables are harder to match than meat because they vary so much: bitter greens, sweet roots, earthy mushrooms, tangy tomatoes. The common thread is freshness, which is why the pick is a bright white or a light red.

With asparagus, peas and green vegetables

Green vegetables, asparagus, peas, courgettes and soft herbs above all, share flavour compounds with Sauvignon Blanc, and a Sancerre or a Marlborough Sauvignon picks up those green notes rather than clashing with them. Grüner Veltliner, with its white-pepper edge, is one of the few wines that handles asparagus and artichoke gracefully.

With salads

Salads dressed with vinaigrette need high acidity in the wine, or the dressing will make it taste flat; Albariño, Assyrtiko, Verdejo and Vinho Verde are all sharp enough.

With roasted vegetables and mushrooms

Roasted and grilled vegetables develop sweetness and char, and they welcome a light red: a Beaujolais, a Chinon with its leafy edge, Barbera or Cinsault, served a little cool. Mushrooms, lentils and earthier dishes can take a warmer Grenache. A dry Provence rosé suits a table of mezze, ratatouille or grilled peppers. Vegetable dishes heavy with cheese or cream follow the cheese or the cream rather than the vegetable. What rarely works is heavy tannin or heavy oak; with no protein or fat to soften them, both taste coarse.
