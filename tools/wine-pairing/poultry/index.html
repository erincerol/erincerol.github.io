Why these wines work with poultry

Chicken, turkey and duck are among the most accommodating things you can put on a plate, which is why the list for poultry is long and runs from whites to light reds. The meat itself is mild; what decides the wine is the cooking and the sauce. A simple roast chicken wants a white with body, a white Burgundy, a Chenin Blanc from Vouvray or a waxy Semillon, because its weight matches the crisp skin and the juices without overpowering the flesh. Cream and butter sauces push you further that way, and a full Viognier, Marsanne or Roussanne holds up beautifully. Go the other direction, with mushrooms, bacon or a red wine sauce, and a soft red makes more sense: Pinot Noir, a Beaujolais served lightly chilled, or a Cabernet Franc from Chinon. Duck is richer and fattier than other birds and can carry more, a red Burgundy or a supple Merlot, while fruit sauces such as orange or cherry favour a red with bright acidity. Turkey at Christmas, surrounded by stuffing, sprouts and cranberry, is a case for Pinot Noir or a juicy, low-tannin Zweigelt. Spice-rubbed or barbecued chicken suits an off-dry Riesling or a cold rosé. The thing to avoid is heavy tannin; lean white meat has little fat to soften it, and a big young red will make the bird taste dry.
