Why these wines work with pork

Pork sits between white meat and red, and it pairs that way: a red without too much tannin, or a rich white. The meat is sweet and fairly fatty, especially belly and shoulder, so wines with good acidity cut through it and keep each mouthful fresh. Heavy tannin is unnecessary and can taste harsh against that sweetness, which is why the reds on this list are the soft and juicy ones: Pinot Noir, Gamay, Barbera, Dolcetto, Zweigelt and Blaufrankisch. A roast loin with crackling suits a Beaujolais or a Chinon; a slow-cooked shoulder with smoky, sticky flavours can take a riper Zinfandel or Pinotage. On the white side, pork’s natural partner is fruit, apple and pear above all, and whites that taste of the orchard with good acidity feel made for it: Chenin Blanc from Vouvray, a Riesling from the Mosel or Alsace, or a peppery Gruner Veltliner. Pork in cream and mustard wants something fuller, Chardonnay or Viognier. Sausages and charcuterie lean towards Italian reds such as Barbera or Montepulciano, whose acidity cuts the salt and fat. Chinese and Southeast Asian pork dishes, sticky and sweet with a touch of chilli, are where an off-dry Riesling or a Gewurztraminer shines, the sweetness in the glass matching the glaze. A dry rosé is an easy bridge for anything that comes off the grill.
