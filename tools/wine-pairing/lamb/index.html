Why these wines work with lamb

Lamb has a sweetness and a gentle gaminess that young, fiercely tannic reds can trample, which is why the pick here is a red with some years on it. Time in bottle softens tannin and draws out savoury, leathery and dried-herb notes, and those meet lamb’s own flavour halfway. A Rioja that has spent years in barrel and bottle is the classic match for a roast leg with rosemary, and a mature left-bank Bordeaux does much the same for a rack. Lamb is fattier than it looks, though, especially shoulder and chops, so there is still room for structure: Syrah, Mourvedre and Grenache from the south of France, Xinomavro and Agiorgitiko from Greece, or Okuzgozu and Bogazkere from Anatolia, where lamb is the everyday meat and the local reds grew up beside it. Herbs and garlic push lamb towards wines with a savoury, scrubby edge rather than lush fruit. Spiced dishes such as a tagine or kofta suit a warm, generous Grenache or a Châteauneuf-du-Pape, whose ripeness meets the spice without fighting it. Slow-cooked shoulder, falling apart after hours in the oven, wants less tannin than a pink-roasted rack; the meat is already soft, so the wine can be too. If all you have is a young, firm red, open it an hour or two ahead and let the air do some of the work the years would have done.
