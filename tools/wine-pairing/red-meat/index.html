Why these wines work with steak and red meat

Red meat is rich with fat and protein, and that is exactly what tannin is for. The grip you feel on your gums from a young Cabernet Sauvignon, a Malbec or a Tannat comes from compounds that bind to protein; meet them with a steak and they latch onto the meat instead, so the wine tastes rounder and the beef tastes cleaner.

With steak and a charred rib

Fat works the other way, coating the mouth so the next sip feels softer than the last. That is why the list here leans dark and structured: Syrah with its pepper, Nebbiolo and Aglianico with their firm tannin, a left-bank Bordeaux with some age. Weight matters as much as grip. A charred rib has real flavour, and a light red simply disappears beside it, so you want a wine that holds its own on the palate.

With a braise, a ragù or a pan sauce

Acidity earns its place with tomato-based ragùs or anything finished with a pan sauce, which is where Sangiovese and Tempranillo come in. Softer choices such as Merlot or Grenache suit leaner cuts and gentler cooking, where the meat has less fat to tame the tannin. Lamb and game follow the same logic, each with a twist of its own.

What to avoid

Steer away from a big, oaky red with a lean, well-done cut; with nothing to soften it, the tannin turns drying and faintly bitter. Rarer meat, more fat and a heavier hand with the seasoning all call for more structure in the glass.
