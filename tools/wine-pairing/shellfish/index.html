Why these wines work with shellfish

Shellfish tastes of the sea, and the wines that suit it best taste as though they were made within sight of it: a crisp white with salt in it. Oysters, mussels, clams, prawns and crab are sweet, briny and delicate, so you want high acidity, little or no oak and a mineral, almost saline edge.

With oysters

Muscadet is the classic with oysters, bone-dry and briny after resting on its lees. Albariño from Rías Baixas, Assyrtiko from Santorini, Vermentino and Vinho Verde come from coastlines too, and that salinity shows in the glass. Chablis, lean and stony, is the textbook choice for a seafood platter, and Champagne with oysters is a cliché because it is right.

With prawns, mussels and clams

Sauvignon Blanc from Sancerre or Marlborough suits shellfish with herbs, garlic or a sharp dressing, and a dry, cold sherry is a remarkable match for prawns and anything fried in batter. Sparkling wine works across the board because its bubbles and acidity scrub the palate clean between mouthfuls.

With lobster, scallops and crab

Richer preparations change the picture. Lobster, scallops seared in butter or crab in a creamy sauce want more body, a white Burgundy, a Semillon or a perfumed Viognier, much as a buttery piece of fish does. Red wine is the one thing to keep away, since tannin and the iodine in shellfish together taste of metal.
